# How Scores Are Calculated

This page explains exactly how Borderless AI gives you a visa score — so you can trust the number and understand what it means.

---

## The Big Rule: No Made-Up Scores

The AI **never invents a score.** Every point in your score comes from a real, fixed visa rule — the same way a government immigration officer would check it.

The AI only explains the score in plain English. It cannot change it.

---

## Three Things That Make Up Your Score

### 1. Eligibility Fit
This measures whether you meet the hard requirements.

**Example:** Canada Express Entry requires you to have at least 1 year of work experience. If you don't have it, you don't qualify — it doesn't matter how high your English score is.

### 2. Profile Strength
This measures how competitive you are compared to other applicants.

**Example:** Having a PhD gives you more points than a Bachelor's degree. Having a job offer is worth more than not having one. These are points you can earn — the higher, the better.

### 3. Evidence Quality
This measures how reliable the sources are behind the result. If we found official government documents that directly confirm your case, the confidence is high. If the rules are unclear or the data is old, we'll say so.

---

## Hard Blockers vs Soft Points

Some rules are absolute — we call these **hard requirements**. If you fail one, you're marked as **BLOCKED** for that visa. Soft points cannot override a hard blocker.

**Example — BLOCKED:**
> You want to apply for the US H-1B visa but you don't have a job offer from a US employer. No matter how high your English score or education, you're blocked. We'll tell you this clearly instead of giving you a false high score.

**Example — POINTS-BASED:**
> You want to apply for Canada Express Entry. You have a Bachelor's degree (+21 pts), 3 years of experience (+21 pts), IELTS 7 (+16 pts), and you're 28 years old (+110 pts). Total = eligible.

## The What-If Simulator

After your assessment, you can try changing your inputs to see how your score improves.

Here's how it works behind the scenes:
1. We look at what's missing from your profile
2. We calculate the ROI of fixing each gap — which improvement gives you the biggest score jump for the least time and money
3. We pick the best action and show you: *"If you do X, your score goes from 68 → 83"*

**Example:** "If you improve your French to CLB 7, you'd gain +15 points in Canada Express Entry."

## Score Meanings

| Score | What It Means |
|---|---|
| **86–100** | Exceptional — very rare. Only the strongest profiles reach here (usually with a job offer or nomination). |
| **70–85** | Strong — realistic. You have a good shot under current immigration rules. |
| **50–69** | Possible — but competitive. You meet most criteria but may face waiting or extra conditions. |
| **30–49** | Weak — consider improving your profile before applying. |
| **0–29** | Very unlikely — major changes needed to qualify. |

---

## Why Is My Score Honest?

Many immigration advisors give optimistic scores to win business. We do the opposite:

- We use **refusal-calibrated** scoring — scores are set to reflect what actually happens in real immigration decisions
- We **block** you if a hard requirement is not met, instead of hiding it in fine print
- We show you **exactly which rule** gave you which points
- We show **confidence levels** — if we're not sure, we say so

---

## Full Transparency

Every point in your score is traceable. If you ask "why did I get 68 points?", the system can show you:
- Age: +25 pts
- Education (Bachelor's): +21 pts
- Work Experience (3 yrs): +21 pts
- English (IELTS 7): +16 pts
- No job offer: 0 pts (not penalized, but no bonus)
- **Total: 83 pts**

Nothing is hidden.
